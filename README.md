# Padworks: O&G Completions Simulator

Interactive 3D equipment library and stage-cycle simulator for oil and gas well completions. Built for training. Not for operational decisions. Written, modeled, and coded independently from public sources.

Live at https://padworks.vercel.app (GitHub repository joshlambert13-bot/padworks). The local folder may still be named completions-explorer; the folder name has no effect on the build.

Drop 41: instancing. The pad's repeated units no longer cost a draw call per mesh per unit. `Instanced` renders a unit once as a hidden template, bakes the template's meshes into one geometry per material and record name, and draws every unit as one InstancedMesh with a matrix per unit: 13 pump bodies, 8 frac tanks (as four cream-and-white pairs), the fuel gas trailers, the parked pickups, the 24 sand boxes, and the 10 light towers. Hover and click still open the right record because each instanced mesh carries the group's name. The parts that move, light up, or differ per unit (pump fans, status lamps, exhaust plumes, pump numbers) are drawn per unit on top. Pixels are identical; the draw-call count at the pad overview drops by about half, which is what a laptop GPU or a phone feels.

Drop 42: shareable job links. A Share button on the Simulator toolbar copies a link that opens the job as it is now: basin and setup, pad, phase, stage, time of day, season, and camera preset, packed into one URL-safe token. Opening the link rebuilds the job from the setup and plays the phase and stage forward, so a trainee lands in the scenario rather than at the setup screen. The token holds no account data and no score; progress stays with the account that opens it.

Drop 40: water by basin. Each basin now has its own water source, from what the plays use: the Permian, Eagle Ford, and Haynesville pull from a lined, bermed pit off the pad's west edge with a floating pump skid (pontoons, engine, pump, suction into the water), a bank pump, and a lay-flat line into the pad; Appalachia, the DJ, and the Anadarko use two above-ground storage tanks with a base manifold; the Bakken and the Powder River keep frac tanks and add a frac heater on the discharge run, whose stack steams in winter. The pit freezes over in a deep-snow winter. The pad grades out to take the pit when the basin has one.

Drop 39: winter. A Summer/Winter button next to the time of day. Snow is a shader patch, not geometry: every standard material blends toward snow white where its shaded normal faces up, by a shared uniform, so the pad, every tank top, every hood, handrail, and pipe takes snow at once with no new draw calls; the downhole section and the library viewer stay bare. How much snow is the basin's: deep in the Bakken and the Powder River, most of the way in Appalachia and the DJ, a light frost in the Permian and almost none in the Eagle Ford and Haynesville. Winter also brings an overcast sky with twice the cloud cover, a weak white sun, close pale haze, flurries around the camera (one draw call), and exhaust that condenses into bigger whiter plumes.

Drop 38: flags. Two flagpoles at the data van: the US flag in the position of honor and the basin's state flag beside it (Texas, North Dakota, Louisiana, Pennsylvania, Colorado, Oklahoma, Wyoming, Ohio). All drawn in code: the US flag exactly, each state flag with its correct field, colors, and layout, and the complex seals (the pelican, the eagle, the coat of arms, the Osage shield, the bison, Ohio's burgee) as simplified silhouettes. No files, nothing licensed.

Drop 37: light plants. Ten towers placed by work area instead of four at the corners: both ends of the wellhead row, both ends of the pump row, the sand and water side, the hydration and chemical side, the data van, and the flowback spread, each turned so its lamps face its work area, each with its own aimed spot light at night.

Found and fixed: the fuel gas trailers parked with their outside tires inside the pad berm (moved 3 m in). Checked and clean: crane hook and bail, wireline run from drum to grease head, tree hydraulic hoses to the stands, pump discharge iron and suction hoses to the missile, zipper legs to the trees, flowback line to the spread, the water transfer run, the data van, power generation, and the flagpoles against the data van. Drop 35: sand to the blender. The pad setup has a sand delivery choice (basin default: boxes in the Permian, Eagle Ford, and DJ; silos elsewhere). With silos, the silo bank and its belt feed the blender hopper as before. With boxes, a box station stands in the same place: two cradles over the collecting belt, each a steel frame holding a box with a discharge hopper under its gate, feeding the same incline and chute into the hopper; the box stack sits to the west, and the container handler runs a real route, a full box on the forks along the lane in front of the stack, a turn at the corner, up to the first cradle to set it down, then around and back empty to pick up the next. The handler follows a polyline with its heading blended through the corners, forks leading on both legs.

Drop 34: two physical fixes. The container handler's yaw was 90 degrees off, so it crabbed sideways with the forks across its path; it now drives forks-first both ways and turns around at the ends. The wireline crane's hook block was placed inside the crane's group with coordinates meant for the trailer's frame, so it hung 6 m west and 3 m south of the boom tip in midair, and the unit itself parked 10 m down the row past the next wellhead. The unit now parks east of its own well with the crane in line with the well, the turret slewed toward it with the counterweight behind the boom, the boom tip plumb over the lubricator, and the hook in a lifting bail on top of the grease head with the wireline's top sheave hung off the hook block.

Drop 33: fracture height growth. The fracture wings used to be drawn at a fixed height through every bed; now they stay in the target band until net pressure exceeds the upper limestone's contrast (700 psi), work through the barrier over the next 450 psi, run through the sand above once they reach it (1,150 psi), and stop at the next shale unless pressure climbs past 2,200 psi; the lower barrier is a little stronger, so growth is biased upward. Viscous fluids grow height faster for the same pressure. Each stage records the height it has reached, so a wing keeps its height after the pressure comes off and settled stages show what happened to them. A normal stage ends just inside the upper barrier; pumping past design with sand on, or a screenout, puts it out of zone into the sand above, which costs 5 points (`outOfZone`), logs the stage and net pressure, and reads on the frac card as "Frac height: Out of zone". The bed stack and the contrasts live in one place (`src/sim/geology.js`) so the height model and the picture cannot disagree. The numbers are training weights, not a stress log.

Drop 32: sound. A Sound/Muted button on the Simulator toolbar (off by default; browsers only start audio after a click, and the choice is remembered). Everything is synthesized in Web Audio from oscillators and filtered noise, so there are no audio files to license, host, or download: a wind bed; the pump fleet as a rumble and throb that follow the slurry rate (idle when the pumps are online at zero rate); the wireline unit's whine while the cable is moving; the coiled tubing injector and mill; flowback choke hiss by choke opening; a two-tone alarm pulse while any alarm is latched; and one-shots for valve actuation, an interlock rejection, guns firing, a plug setting, and a misfire. The engine subscribes to the sim store and moves gains and pitches when state changes, so nothing runs per frame and the frame budget is untouched. Leaving the simulator mutes it; coming back re-arms it on the next click.

Drop 31: night. The time-of-day button now cycles Day, Dusk, Night. Night puts a moon 18 degrees up in the east-southeast (the orbit camera cannot look above level, so a high moon would never be seen) as a weak bluish key light with the same view-following shadows, a star field of 1,400 screen-size points on the dome (one draw call), the sky, haze, clouds, and ground pulled down to night values derived from each basin's own palette, lit lamp heads on the four light towers with a shadowless spot light at each one aimed in toward the working area, and the data van's window band lit from inside. The spot lights exist only while night is on, since every material pays for them per fragment: on the software renderer used for the headless checks a night frame costs about a fifth more than a day frame (2.4 s against 2.0 s in Lite at the pad overview); a GPU barely notices. The first switch to night recompiles the materials, a short hitch, after which the cost is steady.

Drop 30: time of day, and the downhole section drawn the way a section is drawn. A Day/Dusk button on the Surface toolbar swaps the sun for a low western one (10 degrees up, warm, weaker), tints the sky, haze, clouds, ground, and caliche toward evening, lowers the exposure a notch, and lights the four light towers. The shadows go long across the pad because the same view-following shadow camera simply follows the new sun, so the frame cost is the same as midday. The sun's glow is now an additive sprite (it can only brighten the sky; the old translucent sphere darkened it). Downhole, the target band is no longer a box set back behind the casing: it is extruded with a half-round notch of the hole radius, so rock meets cement and the section face is at the cut plane like every other bed; the casing wall and cement annulus show as strips along the cut edges so the section reads as solid steel and cement rather than two thin shells. The perforating cluster now fires twelve shots at 60 degree phasing along two turns of the gun spiral and draws them as a cutaway shows them: the shots in the section plane as sectioned tunnels through the face with a crushed zone at the mouth, the shots into the far half as holes on the inside of the casing, and the shots into the removed half not at all. The old six tunnels were not radial and three of them hung in the air.

Drop 29: downhole realism, and the cutaway that was not cutting away. Since Drop 20 the casing has been the back half of a cylinder so the camera looks into the trough, but the target shale band's section face sat at the same plane and hid it: what read as the bore was the rock face with particles and tools drawn on top. The band is now as tall as the borehole and its box stops 0.7 m behind the section plane, so the cemented casing, couplings, plugs, sleeves, and the fluid are seen inside the trough as intended. Rock textures are 512 px with structure: shale with fine wavy laminae, fissility breaks, and silt bands; sandstone with grain and cross-bedded sets between flat set boundaries; limestone with nodular mottling, stylolites, and sparse vertical joints. Fluid in the bore is a translucent column filling the casing from the heel to where the fluid is going, turbulence scrolling along it at the pump rate (blue for slickwater, tan for slurry), with the particles stretched into streaks by velocity on top. Proppant is instanced grains with per-grain color packed into the fracture wing instead of point sprites.

Drop 28: pad life and the water transfer line. The frac tanks were not connected to anything, so the hydration unit had no water: there is now a suction header on stands along the tank fronts fed by a short hose from each tank's manifold, a diesel transfer pump trailer at the row end (engine box with louvers and exhaust, centrifugal pump, suction from the header), and a blue lay-flat discharge hose on the ground across the pad to the hydration unit's suction manifold, with a round riser to the stub. Two crew members walk: one from the data van to the accumulator stand and back along the red zone edge, one patrolling the frac tanks; legs and arms swing from hip and shoulder pivots and they face the way they go. The static forklift at the sand boxes is now a container handler shuttling along the front of the rows, a full box out and empty back, wheels turning, mast tilting, with a hydraulic lift cylinder.

Drop 27: Lite quality stops rendering metal black. Lite (phones by default, the Lite button, slow machines after a context loss) has no environment map, so bare metal with nothing to reflect rendered as black silhouettes: pump castings, flanges, iron. Two fixes by cost. On the pad and in the downhole section, Lite now caps metalness at 0.35 and lifts roughness, so direct and hemisphere light shade steel as dull gray with highlights; the frame cost is unchanged. In the library viewer, where one model fills few pixels, Lite builds the dome's sky straight into a small float texture with an HDR sun, runs it through PMREM once, and uses it as the environment, so the model gets real reflections. (The same environment was tried on the whole pad: it looked best, but sampling it on every pixel made Lite frames 75 percent slower under a software renderer, which is the machine Lite exists for.)

Drop 26: wireline and coiled tubing units. The wireline truck now backs up to the well the way it is worked: drum at the rear facing the tree, measuring head sheave on the rear post, the line running from the drum over the measuring head, up to a top sheave hanging from the crane's hook block, and straight down into the grease head. The drum spins about its own axis (the old version tumbled), its wound cable thins as the tool string runs in and thickens as it pulls out, the level wind traverses with the line, and the line itself visibly runs over the sheaves. The drum has flanges with lightening holes, bearing caps, a hydraulic drive motor with hoses to the cab. The crane has rectangular telescoping boom sections, a lift cylinder with a chrome rod, jack cylinders on the outriggers, a counterweight, a glazed operator cab, a boom-tip sheave block, and a hook block. The coiled tubing reel has spoked flanges, a hub, a wound tubing layer that thins as pipe goes in the hole, a level wind traversing the reel face, the drive motor with chain guard and hoses to the power pack (which has louvers), and the tubing runs as a moving line from the reel over the gooseneck into the injector; a four-hose bundle hangs from the cabin to the injector drive motors and stripper. Partner wells' wireline units pay out and take up on their own. Budget: see the modeling standard; the moving lines are one draw call each.

Drop 25: shadows that follow the view, and pump unit and trailer detail. The sun's shadow frustum now follows the orbit target and shrinks as the camera comes in, so the same shadow map that covered the whole pad at 10 cm per texel gives centimeter shadows at a valve or a pump close-up; the frustum center snaps to the texel grid so panning does not make shadow edges swim. Pump units: diesel enclosures have louvered side panels, panel seams, a door outline with a latch; electric units have cabinet doors with seams, handles, and hinges on both sides and louvers on the back; every unit has a hose reel on the gooseneck deck and a fire extinguisher by the control panel. Every trailer (pumps, blender, hydration, chemical unit, data van, fuel trailers, missile) has mud flaps behind the rear axle, an air tank and a tool box under the deck, red and white conspicuity tape along both sides, amber marker lamps along the deck edge, and red markers at the rear. Tires have a tread pattern (bump and tone) and 20 facets instead of 14. All of it rides in the meshes the units already had (the markings are one vertex-colored mesh per trailer), so the budget at the pad overview went from 659k to 718k rendered triangles at 3,193 to 3,198 draw calls, and at close views the following shadow frustum culls more from the shadow pass than before; the frame-time guard is unchanged.

Drop 24: wellhead and zipper detail. Every flange pair on the tree, the wellhead side outlets, the zipper legs, the riser spools, and the flanged treating spools carries studs with hex nuts at both ends (instanced, two draw calls per group). Gate valves have a nameplate on the body, grease and sealant injection fittings on the bonnet, a packing gland nut under the handwheel on manual valves, a handwheel hub, and on hydraulic valves end caps on the actuator cylinder and a fitting block with two elbows where the control hoses leave; the hoses now hang by their span instead of a fixed sag. Hammer unions on the pump iron and the treating line show the wing nut over the male sub collar and the female sub shoulder. The treating line from the missile and the flowback line from wing B stand on pipe stands (base plate, post, saddle) instead of floating. Budget: 538k to 659k rendered triangles, 3,130 to 3,193 draw calls at the pad overview.

Drop 23: surface realism, first round. Painted bodies (cabs, cabinets, hoods, tank walls, totes, generator and switchgear housings, pump castings) are rounded-edge boxes instead of sharp blocks, and paint is a clearcoat material in Full quality: a glossy coat over a worn base, with a dirt map, a light bump, and a shared wear map so panels catch the sky differently. Frac tanks, the hydration tank, and the sand silos carry dust and rust streaks running down from the seams and a dirt band along the bottom. Bare steel and aluminum have a brushed roughness map so reflections smear. The pad surface has pebbles and small stones over the gravel noise. Mesas on the horizon are a stepped-cliff profile with talus and strata instead of flat-shaded prisms; ridges are lumpy domes with the same rock; a few cumulus patches drift above the horizon. Everything is still generated in the browser from noise (no image files), and the triangle count grew about 11 percent with the same draw calls, so the frame-time budget and the Effects guard are unchanged. Lite quality keeps the standard material without clearcoat or bump.

Drop 22: admin view switch, error reports, and the camera following the steps. An admin can look at the site as a trainee or as an instructor would see it (user menu, View the site as); a banner across the top says which view is on, with a button back to the admin view. The mask only hides the admin and instructor parts of the interface; the account's rights on the server are unchanged. Report an error in this record (bottom of every library record) now opens a short form that saves the report with the record ID, the open tab, and who sent it; reports show in the Accounts page under Error reports, where an admin resolves or deletes them (instructors can read them). Pressing a step's button in the Next steps card or a lesson card glides the surface camera to the equipment the step is about: a tree valve at its own height from the operator side, the zipper leg, the missile inlet, the pump row, the wireline or coiled tubing unit, the flowback spread, the workover rig. Mouse or touch takes over as usual afterwards.

Drop 21: pad corrections, second round. Remote hydraulic stands: one per tree-and-leg pair (eight valves each: five on the tree, two on the zipper leg), on an arc in front of the accumulator unit, supply daisy-chained from the skid stand to stand, each stand's control hoses running on the ground to its tree trunk and its leg trunk; the operator stands at the accumulator panel, outside the red zone. Pump iron connected: each pump's discharge swivel arm now runs from the missile feed port to the end of the pump's own discharge pipe, and the suction hose from the low-pressure outlet to the pump's suction hose at ground level. The silo incline lands on the blender hopper through a discharge chute. Frac tanks have their crowned roof the right way up, the walkway and both handrails on the crown, and stairs from the ground at the front end. The red zone is an L-shaped outline around the missile and discharge side plus the treating line, zipper, and wells, so the blender, hydration, and chemical units behind the missile are outside it; the wireline and coiled tubing units sit outside its edge; signs at four corners. Pickups are a generic white three-quarter-ton crew cab (rounded sheet metal, four doors, tow mirrors, no badge or grille signature) parked in a row and on the lease road, where the truck now rides the ground on a graded road corridor instead of passing through the terrain.

Drop 20: sleeve job animation and the casing cutaway turned to face you. The downhole casing, cement, couplings, sleeve housings, and packers now keep their back half instead of their top half, so the camera in front looks into the trough and every tool, ball, plug, and particle sits against the wall instead of under a pipe. Sliding sleeve jobs animate: the frac ball rolls and tumbles down the lateral with the fluid, lands on the seat, and the inner sleeve shears and shifts toe-ward over half a second while the steel covers slide off the port windows and a flash marks the shear; the toe sleeve pressure-up glows the bore from blue to orange with the pressure fraction until the ports open the same way. On dissolvable systems the plug (or the ball on its seat) shrinks in place while it sheds a stream of fines that drifts heel-ward, instead of a blue bubble.

Drop 19: postprocessing. Full quality now runs screen-space ambient occlusion (N8AO) and bloom through an effect composer in all three scenes: pad, downhole, and the record viewer. Occlusion settles parts onto decks and the ground and darkens the creases between them; bloom lifts only what is brighter than white (the sun disk, the pressure glow in the fractures, the perforating flash, mill sparks), so labels and paint stay crisp. An Effects button sits beside Full in the simulator toolbar. A frame-time guard watches the first seconds with the effects on and turns them off for the session when the machine averages more than 45 ms per frame, with the measured figure in the button's tooltip; the button turns them back on. Lite mode never mounts the composer. Three new dependencies (@react-three/postprocessing, postprocessing, n8ao), all free and local to the build.

Drop 18: downhole animation. The section view now shows the work instead of jumping between states. Plug setting is a sequence on the wireline string: run in with everything retracted, the setting tool strokes, the slips ride their cones and bite the casing, the element squeezes out against the wall, the shear stud lets go, and the string backs off half a meter with the plug left set. Perforating fires one cluster at a time, toe first, over about two seconds of job time, each with a short flash and six shaped-charge jets at the gun phasing that fade as the tunnels appear. Drillout: composite cuttings tumble heel-ward along the low side of the annulus with a slow spiral while the mill throws sparks, and a cuttings bed builds on the low side from the heel out to the toe-most milled plug; the camera follows the mill from plug to plug. Flowback: produced fluid comes out of every open cluster and runs to the heel, tan sand grains among the water early in the cleanup and clean returns as the recovered volume rises, while the cuttings bed shortens and thins; fractures lose their pressure glow once pumping is over and read as faint propped planes. Pump-down fluid moves with the string during a plug and perf run.

Drop 17: library viewer hero pass. The record models no longer render as flat facets: normals are rebuilt with a 32 degree crease so cylinders and fillets shade smoothly while machined edges stay crisp. Each part's material is classed from its export color (cast steel, bright machined steel, brass and bronze bushings, elastomer seals, paint) and lit by the same sky environment as the pad, with key, fill, and rim lights, a studio gradient backdrop, and a ground that catches the model's shadow. The model turns slowly on a turntable until it is touched; hovering a part (in the scene or in the parts list) lights it, the selected part carries a floating label, and section mode draws the cut faces as solid material instead of the hollow inside of a shell.

Drop 16: accounts and admin, plus pad layout corrections. The whole site is behind a sign-in: an admin creates each account (username, display name, role) and hands the trainee a one-time password; the trainee chooses a password at first sign-in. Roles: admin (manage accounts, see everything), instructor (see everyone's progress, no account changes), trainee. Lesson results and job summaries are saved to the trainee's account on the server (no more browser-only results); the admin panel shows per-trainee lesson bests, attempts, time on task, last sign-in, every saved job summary (opened in the same session summary view), a CSV export, password reset, disable, role change, and delete. The backend is one Vercel serverless function (`api/index.js`, routed by `server/api.js`) on Neon Postgres, with bcrypt password hashes, signed httpOnly cookie sessions, a sign-in rate limit, and no email or tracking; the headless checks run against the same code on an in-process Postgres (PGlite). Pad: wellheads now sit below grade in cellars (casing head, casing spool with side outlet valves, and tubing head in a square pit with a curb; the tree starts at grade), the blender moved behind the missile so its suction hoses reach the low-pressure headers without crossing the pumps, with hydration, chemical, and silo units beside it and the silo incline landing on the blender hoppers; the frac valve control unit is now the accumulator unit on its own skid outside the red zone, with the tree and zipper hydraulic trunks bundled on the ground to it; the data van no longer overlaps the fuel gas trailers (moved to the entrance side); the water tanks and a light tower no longer overlap; the red zone is drawn as a boundary around the missile, treating line, zipper, and wells with signs at the corners. See "Accounts" below for the one-time Vercel setup.

Drop 15: downhole rendering pass. The section view gets the treatment the surface got: each bed is a textured rock (grainy sandstone, laminated shale, jointed limestone; generated in the browser and tinted by the bed color, with bump in full quality), the cement sheath is grainy, the casing is metallic under a dim underground environment map with soft shadows and a cool fill light, plugs, tool strings, coil, and packers cast shadows on the casing, fracture wings have irregular edges from a noise alpha map and a stronger pressure glow, proppant is drawn as round sand grains with per-grain color, fluid particles glow a little more and stay inside the beds, and the schematic grid shows only with labels. Drop 14: vegetation and terrain. Plants are built per basin from a few instanced parts with per-plant color variation: pines as trunk and three cone tiers, hardwoods as trunk, two branches, and a lumpy three-blob canopy, mesquite as a leaning trunk under a wide flat canopy, creosote scrub and sage as low clumps, brush as a bushy mid-size plant, and prairie grass as alpha-tested crossed-quad tufts; forested basins carry up to 1,700 plants in full quality (550 in lite). Instanced rocks, a 2 km outer ground plane, and a horizon ring that fits the basin: mesas and buttes for the desert, a pointed pine or rounded hardwood tree line at the clearing edge, rolling ridges for the plains. The lease road now runs out 320 m with a barbed-wire fence on both sides, a cattle guard and gate posts at the pad entrance, and two gate signs (check in, H2S and no smoking). New camera preset: Pad entrance. Terrain math moved to src/sim/parts/terrain.js.

Drop 12: rendering pass. A procedural sky dome (basin sky, haze, and ground colors with a sun) is both the background and, rendered once into a small cube map, the image-based light for every surface, so bare metal reflects the sky and paint takes sky light; materials reworked (metallic steel, aluminum, and brass; dielectric paint with a shared wear map that varies roughness; rubber, tires, glass); soft shadows with bias tuning; ACES tone mapping with rebalanced lights; ground and pad textures generated in the browser (fractal noise as color and bump on the terrain, gravel with wheel ruts and stains on the pad and the lease road); blob contact shadows under every trailer, tank, silo, sand box row, and wellhead; the library viewer lit by the same sky with self-shadowing and rubber parts detected by color. Drop 10: scoring and guided lessons. ## Run it

Requires Node.js 22 (LTS). Inside this folder:

```
npm install
npm run build
npm run serve
```

Open http://localhost:4173. `npm run serve` serves the build and the accounts API with Postgres in process (PGlite, memory only: accounts vanish when it stops). The local admin is `admin` / `padworks-admin`; the first sign-in asks for a new password. For live editing run `npm run api` in one terminal and `npm run dev` in another (the dev server proxies `/api` to the API on port 4174).

On a phone on the same Wi-Fi: run `npm run serve` and open http://<your computer's address>:4173.

## Accounts

Production needs three things in the Vercel project, once:

1. A database: Vercel dashboard, the project, Storage tab, Create Database, Neon (free). Connecting it to the project adds `DATABASE_URL` automatically. The tables are created on the first request.
2. `PADWORKS_SESSION_SECRET`: Settings, Environment Variables, any phrase of 16 characters or more. Signs the session cookies; changing it signs everyone out.
3. `PADWORKS_ADMIN_USER` and `PADWORKS_ADMIN_PASSWORD`: the first admin account, created when the database has no users. The password is one-time: the first sign-in asks for a new one. After that these two variables can stay or go.

Then Redeploy. Sign in, open Accounts, create accounts, hand out the one-time passwords. What is stored: username, display name, role, password hash, lesson results, job summaries, error reports (record ID, page, message, who sent it), sign-in failure timestamps (for the rate limit). No email, no cookies beyond the session, no analytics.

## Review workflow

Every AI-drafted record is `status: "draft"` and is labeled "Unreviewed draft" on the site. To review a record: open the JSON, read every tab, check each citation against the source register, correct the text, then set `status` to `screened` (read once) or `reviewed` (every tab and source checked) and add yourself to `reviewers`:

```json
"status": "reviewed",
"reviewers": [{ "name": "J. Lambert", "affiliation": "Independent", "date": "2026-10-01", "scope": "all tabs" }]
```

`verified` requires two independent sources per numeric value. `npm run validate` refuses a non-draft record with no reviewer.

## Geometry

Two paths, both free:

1. Procedural (Three.js in code): everything in the simulator scenes (`src/sim/parts/`). Node names equal record IDs; a record with `"scene": "WH-FRACTREE"` binds to that node.
2. Scripted CAD (CadQuery): the scripts in `cad/scripts/WH/` build named solids and write uncompressed GLBs into `cad/out/WH/`; `npm run glb` compresses them into `public/glb/WH/`. The shared builders live in `cad/scripts/WH/_lib.py`. You do not need Python on your machine to use the site: the compressed GLBs are already in `public/glb/`. To regenerate, install Python 3.12, run `pip install cadquery trimesh` in a virtual environment, then `python cad/scripts/WH/WH-FRACTREE.py --out cad/out/WH` (and the other scripts: `WH-ZIPPER.py --all`, `PP/PP-MISSILE.py`, `DT/DT-FRACSLEEVE.py`, and the rest), then `npm run glb`. The viewer decodes Draco with the files in `public/draco/`.

## Deploy

Vercel (recommended, free Hobby plan): sign in with GitHub, import the repository, accept the detected Vite settings, deploy, then do the one-time setup under "Accounts" above. `vercel.json` rewrites every path except `/api/*` to `index.html` so record links work; `api/index.js` becomes the one serverless function. Every push to `main` redeploys. Cloudflare Pages and GitHub Pages also work (build `npm run build`, output `dist`; the `404.html` copy handles path routing on GitHub Pages). The step-by-step guide is in the Setup Guide document delivered with Drop 2.

`GITHUB_REPO` in `src/config.js` points the "Report an error" link on each record at the repository's issues page. The logo lives in `public/brand/` (`mark.svg` plus PNG renders).
